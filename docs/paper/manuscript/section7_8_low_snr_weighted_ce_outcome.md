# Section 7.8 Low-SNR Weighted-CE Sensitivity (Loss-Reweighting Intervention Check)

Evidence markers used in this draft: B1 = `PAPER_STAGE6_LOW_SNR_WEIGHTED_CE_3090_REPORT.md`; B2 = `PAPER_STAGE6_LOW_SNR_WEIGHTED_CE_ANALYSIS.md`; B3 = P1.1 evidence (`PAPER_STAGE6_EXTENDED_BUDGET_3090_REPORT.md`); A1 = main_table_metrics.csv; A2 = low_snr_table.csv; P1 = `section4_experimental_protocol.md`; R1 = `section5_1_overall_fixed_split_results.md`; R2 = `section5_2_low_snr_fusion_tradeoff.md`; L1 = `section7_limitations.md`; L7 = `section7_7_training_budget_sensitivity.md`.

## 7.8 Low-SNR Weighted-CE Sensitivity

This subsection records a second separate-evidence-label intervention check, complementary to the budget-sensitivity check in Section 7.7. The intervention replaces plain cross-entropy with a piecewise-constant SNR-group-weighted cross-entropy (low / mid / high weights = 2.0 / 1.0 / 0.7) and otherwise retains the P1.1 extended-budget schedule (epoch 50, 5-epoch warmup, cosine LR, AdamW). It is registered under a new evidence label `LOW_SNR_WEIGHTED_CE_3090` and a separate output root, and it does not modify Stage 5A/5B, P1.1 evidence, or the main results sections. Only the two manuscript-relevant models for low-SNR claims are evaluated: `cldnn` (strongest stable I/Q baseline) and `fusion_iq_stft` (multi-view candidate). Evidence: P1, B1, B2, R2, L7.

The protocol changed in two controlled dimensions relative to P1.1: the loss function and the implicit gradient direction. The hardware (RTX 3090, Ampere sm_86), driver `570.211.01`, CUDA `12.8`, and PyTorch `2.9.1+cu128` remain the same as in P1.1, so the comparison against P1.1 is matched on hardware and on schedule, and only the loss differs. The comparison against Stage 5A inherits the same hardware confound that Section 7.7 already registered. Evidence: B1, B2, B3, L7.

The aggregate behaviour matches the predicted trade-off direction but with a small magnitude. For `cldnn`, the mean overall accuracy across the three retained training seeds was 0.5896 against the Stage 5A 0.6129 and the P1.1 0.6145, for deltas of -0.0233 and -0.0249. The mean low-SNR accuracy was 0.2281 against the Stage 5A 0.2224 and the P1.1 0.2220, for deltas of +0.0058 and +0.0061. The corresponding mid-SNR deltas were -0.0438 and -0.0479, and the high-SNR deltas were -0.0418 and -0.0432. For `fusion_iq_stft`, the same comparisons gave overall 0.5704 against 0.5771 and 0.5851 (deltas -0.0067 and -0.0147), low-SNR 0.2255 against 0.2213 and 0.2124 (deltas +0.0042 and +0.0131), with mid- and high-SNR losses of approximately -1.8 / -3.5 percentage points and -1.0 / -3.2 percentage points respectively. Within this evidence set, low-SNR-weighted CE moves accuracy from mid- and high-SNR examples to low-SNR examples by a small amount, and the size of the loss elsewhere exceeds the size of the gain at low SNR. Evidence: B1, B2.

The per-SNR breakdown of `cldnn` seed 42 illustrates where the gain comes from. Under the weighted loss, the SNR transition zone (-8 to -4 dB) carries most of the change, with -8 dB accuracy moving to 0.395, -6 dB to 0.519, and -4 dB to 0.631. The deep low-SNR samples (-20 to -16 dB) remain at approximately the chance-level accuracy of 1/11 ~ 0.09 across the three new training seeds, indicating that re-weighting alone does not recover signal that the noise has already destroyed. The collapse-to-AM-SSB pattern documented in Section 5.2.A persists at deep low-SNR because the upweighting changes the gradient mass on visible-signal examples, not on noise-dominated examples. Evidence: B2, R2.

The best-epoch distribution under the weighted loss is also informative. `cldnn` reached its best epoch at 23, 15, and 20, similar to its P1.1 distribution (23, 24, 27) and not converging earlier in any pronounced way. `fusion_iq_stft` reached its best epoch at 13, 12, and 13, much earlier than in P1.1 (34, 42, 36). The reading is that the weighted loss accelerates fitting the harder, low-SNR fusion examples and then leaves no further easy gain, which is consistent with the small absolute low-SNR gain combined with the larger mid/high-SNR loss. Evidence: B1, B2, L7.

The supported manuscript reading is therefore narrow. Under the registered protocol, group-based SNR-weighted CE with the (2.0, 1.0, 0.7) weight schedule does not recover the overall-vs-low-SNR trade-off described in Section 5.2; it shifts the trade-off slightly toward low-SNR but at a larger cost in mid- and high-SNR. This conclusion is bounded to the registered loss, the registered weight tuple, and the registered RadioML2016.10A fixed split. It does not generalize to other SNR-aware loss schedules, focal-style weighting, sample-level continuous SNR weighting, per-(class, SNR) weighting, or SNR-balanced samplers. The Stage 6A optimization candidate queue retains those alternatives as future work; the current evidence package does not authorize them. Evidence: B1, B2, R2, L1, L7.

This subsection therefore extends the Section 7 limitations by registering a second sensitivity boundary: the conclusions in Section 5.1, Section 5.2, and Section 7.1 are also robust to the simplest form of SNR-aware loss reweighting, in the sense that this intervention does not produce a model that is both better at low-SNR and at least as good overall on RadioML2016.10A. Combined with the budget-sensitivity check in Section 7.7, this evidence frames the Stage 5A main results as not artifacts of either undertraining or unweighted loss, while explicitly leaving the door open to future SNR-aware techniques whose evaluation is outside the current evidence package. Evidence: P1, R1, R2, L1, L7, B1, B2.
