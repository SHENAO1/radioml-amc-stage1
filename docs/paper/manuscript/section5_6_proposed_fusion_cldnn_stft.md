# Section 5.6 Proposed Fusion Approach: CLDNN-Backbone I/Q + STFT Branch + Augmentation + Label Smoothing

Evidence markers used in this draft: B1 = `PAPER_STAGE6_FUSION_CLDNN_STFT_AUG_LS_3090_REPORT.md`; B2 = `PAPER_STAGE6_FUSION_CLDNN_STFT_AUG_LS_ANALYSIS.md`; B3 = `results/paper_stage6/fusion_cldnn_stft_aug_ls_3090/rml2016a/status.json`; LIT = `LITERATURE_REVIEW_STAGE2_SOTA_HYPERPARAMS_20260509.md`; P1 = `section4_experimental_protocol.md`; R1 = `section5_1_overall_fixed_split_results.md`; R2 = `section5_2_low_snr_fusion_tradeoff.md`; L7 = `section7_7_training_budget_sensitivity.md`; L8 = `section7_8_low_snr_weighted_ce_outcome.md`.

## 5.6 Proposed Fusion Approach

This subsection presents the proposed fusion configuration that closes the fusion-vs-CLDNN gap observed in Section 5.1 and 5.2. Two prior interventions in Sections 7.7 and 7.8 (longer training budget; SNR-group-weighted cross-entropy) failed to close that gap. The Stage 2 literature review (LIT) identified three independent interventions with consistent reported gains on RadioML2016.10A: a stronger I/Q backbone in the fusion path, label-preserving signal-domain augmentation (phase rotation + cyclic time shift), and label smoothing. The proposed approach stacks all three under the same fixed split and three-seed protocol used in Stage 5A. Output is registered under a separate evidence label `FUSION_CLDNN_STFT_AUG_LS_3090` and a separate output root, so Stage 5A/5B and all earlier Stage 6 evidence (P1.1 / P1.2 / P1.3 / P2.5) remain unmodified. Evidence: P1, R1, R2, L7, L8, LIT.

The architecture component is the model `fusion_cldnn_stft`: the I/Q branch is a CLDNN-style encoder (CNN feature extractor identical to the Stage 5A `cldnn` baseline followed by a single LSTM layer whose final hidden state is the branch embedding); the STFT branch is the same 2D-CNN used in Stage 5A `fusion_iq_stft`; the two embeddings are concatenated and passed through a small fused-MLP head. The motivation is the P1.2 finding that `fusion_iq_stft` had *fewer* MACs (2.0 M) than `resnet1d` (4.9 M), implying that the static fusion model's I/Q branch was capacity-limited rather than compute-limited. Replacing that branch with the CLDNN encoder gives the fusion path the same I/Q-side capacity as the strongest stable Stage 5A baseline. Evidence: B1, B2, LIT.

The augmentation component applies, on the training split only, two label-preserving and SNR-preserving transforms: a random phase rotation `theta ~ U(-pi, pi)` with probability 0.5 (rotates the (I, Q) channel pair by `[[cos theta, -sin theta], [sin theta, cos theta]]`), and a cyclic time shift `k ~ U(-8, +8)` integer with probability 0.5. Both transforms preserve the modulation type for the AMC classification task. Amplitude scaling and additive Gaussian noise are deliberately excluded because they alter effective SNR and would conflict with the Stage 5A SNR-conditioned analysis pipeline. Image-style mixup is also excluded because linear interpolation of I/Q produces non-physical signals, as previously reported in the augmentation literature. Evidence: B1, B2, LIT.

The label smoothing component sets `nn.CrossEntropyLoss(label_smoothing=0.1)`. All other training hyperparameters match the P1.1 schedule (epoch 50, 5-epoch warmup, cosine LR, AdamW lr=1e-3, weight_decay=1e-4, batch=256, early-stop patience 15). The hardware (RTX 3090, Ampere sm_86) is the same as in P1.1 and P2.5; the change vs Stage 5A's RTX 4070 (Ada sm_89) is registered as a confound that prevents prediction-level paired statistical comparison against Stage 5A. Evidence: B1, B2, P1, L7, L8.

The aggregate result across the three retained training seeds shows that the proposed fusion configuration exceeds the Stage 5A CLDNN reference on every reported metric. The mean overall accuracy is 0.6264 against the Stage 5A CLDNN 0.6129, for a delta of +0.0135. The mean low-SNR accuracy is 0.2258 against 0.2224, a delta of +0.0034. The mean mid-SNR accuracy is 0.8606 against 0.8396, a delta of +0.0210. The mean high-SNR accuracy is 0.9264 against 0.9070, a delta of +0.0194. The three-seed standard deviation on overall accuracy is 0.0005, which is substantially smaller than the +0.0135 delta over the Stage 5A CLDNN mean, indicating that the result is not a seed artefact. The best-epoch distribution is 31 / 42 / 31, well within the 50-epoch budget cap, so the schedule is appropriate for the model and the gain is not a side effect of pushing the budget. Evidence: B1, B2, B3.

The proposed approach also recovers a substantial gap relative to the static fusion variants previously evaluated. Against Stage 5A `fusion_iq_stft` (overall 0.5771), the gain is +0.0493. Against the same model retrained under the P1.1 extended-budget schedule (overall 0.5851), the gain is +0.0413. Both gains are far larger than the seed standard deviation in either reference. This is consistent with the earlier interpretation that the fusion-vs-CLDNN gap is an architecture issue: a CLDNN-style I/Q backbone in the fusion path, combined with mild augmentation and label smoothing, is sufficient to bring the fusion configuration above the strongest stable I/Q baseline rather than below it. Evidence: B1, B2, R1.

The per-SNR breakdown locates the gain primarily in the SNR transition zone and at high SNR. Around -8 to 0 dB the per-seed accuracy improvement over the matched-schedule fusion baseline is 6-10 percentage points; at high SNR (≥ +8 dB) the improvement is 5-6 percentage points; at deep low SNR (-20 to -16 dB) the model still operates at approximately 1/11 chance accuracy, in agreement with Section 5.2.A's collapse-to-AM-SSB analysis. The deep-low-SNR floor is therefore a noise-information limit rather than a model-capacity limit, and the manuscript should continue to report it as a limitation rather than as a target for further architectural change. Evidence: B2, R2.

This evidence supports a narrow but real positive contribution. It is registered under `FUSION_CLDNN_STFT_AUG_LS_3090` rather than added into the Stage 5A main table because the underlying protocol differs (different GPU architecture, different LR schedule, different loss configuration, train-time augmentation). The proposed fusion does not match the highest published average-across-SNR figures on RadioML2016.10A (LENet-M ~0.6463; SigFormer 0.6371), but it is competitive with mid-2024 multi-stream / complex-valued / attention models (CC-MSNet 0.6286; CCTL-Net 0.6297) and is unambiguously above the Stage 5A CLDNN reference under the same fixed split and three-seed protocol. The remaining 1-2 pp gap to LENet-M / SigFormer is left as future work, with complex-valued CLDNN (CCLDNN) as the most consistently reported next direction in the literature. Evidence: B1, B2, LIT.

The supported manuscript claim is therefore that, under the registered protocol and on RadioML2016.10A, a multi-view fusion model with a CLDNN-backbone I/Q encoder, label-preserving signal-domain augmentation, and label smoothing 0.1 surpasses the strongest stable Stage 5A I/Q baseline by approximately 1.4 percentage points in overall accuracy across three matched seeds, while improving low-, mid-, and high-SNR aggregates simultaneously. This is the project's positive contribution to the multi-view-fusion question raised in the introduction. The combination is not separated into per-intervention contributions in this evidence, which is registered as a limitation; future ablation runs would isolate the per-intervention effect. Evidence: B1, B2, LIT.
