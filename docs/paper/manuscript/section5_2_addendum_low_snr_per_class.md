# Section 5.2 Addendum: Low-SNR Per-Class Confusion Breakdown

Evidence markers used in this draft: A1 = `PAPER_STAGE6_LOW_SNR_CONFUSION_ANALYSIS.md`; A2 = `results/paper_stage6/low_snr_confusion_extended/confusion_low_snr_summary.csv`; A3 = `paper_package/predictions_archive_20260508/`; R2 = `section5_2_low_snr_fusion_tradeoff.md`; P1 = `section4_experimental_protocol.md`.

## 5.2.A Addendum: Per-class low-SNR confusion structure

This addendum supplements Section 5.2 with the per-class breakdown that the Stage 5A statistical-test summary did not include. It is generated by post-processing the 27 archived Stage 5A `predictions_test.csv` files at the protocol-defined low-SNR threshold `snr_db <= -6 dB` and aggregating across the three Stage 5A train seeds. No model is retrained, and the Stage 5A main, low-SNR, paired bootstrap, and McNemar tables remain unchanged. Evidence: A1, A2, A3, P1.

The dominant low-SNR behaviour across the eight stable Stage 5A model rows is collapse to a single predicted class, namely AM-SSB. For each of `cnn1d`, `resnet1d`, `tfcnn_stft`, `fusion_iq_stft`, `cldnn`, `lwamcnet`, `iq_param_matched`, and `gated_fusion_iq_stft`, the five largest off-diagonal cells share the same predicted-class column AM-SSB, with row-fractions in the range 0.628 to 0.805 across the digital-modulation rows (`8PSK`, `BPSK`, `QPSK`, `CPFSK`, `GFSK`). The `mcldnn` row collapses differently because two of its three Stage 5A seeds collapsed to chance-level single-class predictions (Section 5.5); the addendum still includes it so the matrix is complete. Evidence: A1, A2.

The per-class low-SNR accuracy table (A2) shows the same pattern from a different angle. AM-SSB low-SNR accuracy is 0.87 to 0.95 for all stable models, AM-DSB is 0.26 to 0.32, and every other class is below 0.34, with most digital modulations below 0.10. The interpretation for the manuscript is that AM-SSB has a noise-robust spectral signature (single-sideband asymmetry) that survives down to `snr_db <= -6 dB`, while digital modulations lose their constellation-level structure at the same SNR and are therefore predicted as the noise-robust class by default. This is consistent with the chance-level floor of 1/11 ~ 0.09 plus a strong AM-SSB bias rather than uniformly random predictions. Evidence: A1, A2.

The fusion versus parameter-matched-baseline trade-off becomes more interpretable under this breakdown. The Section 5.2 statement that `fusion_iq_stft` has a small low-SNR aggregate gain over `iq_param_matched` (+0.005 in the Stage 5A statistical-test table) decomposes into class-level gains and losses: positive contributions on QAM64 (+0.089 absolute), PAM4 (+0.003 absolute), and 8PSK (+0.022 absolute), and negative contributions on QAM16 (-0.073 absolute) and several smaller cells. The fusion advantage is therefore modulation-specific rather than uniform, and it is concentrated on a handful of digital classes whose constellation structure interacts with the time-frequency view. Evidence: A2, R2.

The CLDNN low-SNR aggregate advantage decomposes similarly. CLDNN's low-SNR accuracy 0.2224 versus `iq_param_matched` 0.2163 (a +0.006 aggregate gap consistent with the Stage 5A bootstrap CI) is dominated by AM-SSB (+0.034 absolute) and AM-DSB (+0.019 absolute), with smaller positive contributions on QAM64 and other classes. The interpretation is that CLDNN's stronger temporal modeling captures slower-varying analog spectra under noise more effectively, not that CLDNN solves the digital-modulation collapse. Evidence: A2.

This per-class structure supports two narrow manuscript additions to Section 5.2 without changing its top-level claims: a sentence that the Stage 5A low-SNR aggregates compress different failure modes into one number, and a sentence that the fusion-versus-parameter-matched gain is concentrated on a small number of digital-modulation classes rather than being uniform. The aggregate-level conclusion in Section 5.2 (no significant fusion advantage over CLDNN at low-SNR; small advantage over `iq_param_matched`; lower overall accuracy) is unchanged. Evidence: A1, A2, R2.

The addendum also surfaces a methodological constraint that the manuscript must respect: any future architectural fix at low-SNR is not credible unless it improves the digital-modulation rows, not just the AM-SSB / AM-DSB rows. The collapse-to-AM-SSB pattern is the actual loss mechanism at low SNR, and an aggregate gain on AM-SSB-heavy classes can mask continued failure on digital modulations. The Stage 6 SNR-aware weighted CE evaluation is therefore registered as a separate evidence label so its low-SNR effect can be reported per class against the same baseline. Evidence: A1, A2.
